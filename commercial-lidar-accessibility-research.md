# Commercial Landscape: LiDAR & Depth-Sensing Accessibility Solutions for Blind and Low-Vision Users

**Research date:** 15 August 2026
**Method:** Every value below was taken from a page fetched during this research session. Source URLs are inline next to each claim. `n.a.` = could not be confirmed from a fetched primary source.

---

## Executive summary of the landscape

**Headline finding: true LiDAR is rare, and where it exists it is mostly a *phone* sensor or a *mapping* tool — not an on-body wearable sensor.**

Four distinct roles for LiDAR/depth sensing emerged:

1. **On-device consumer LiDAR (iPhone/iPad Pro).** Apple's own Door Detection explicitly uses "LiDAR, camera, and on-device machine learning" and requires an iPhone/iPad with the LiDAR Scanner ([Apple Newsroom, May 2022](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2022/05/apple-previews-innovative-accessibility-features/)). A cluster of small third-party iOS apps (Super Lidar, Obstacle Detector, AI Guide Dog, EyeGuide Vision) piggyback on the same LiDAR/TrueDepth hardware and are almost all free.
2. **LiDAR as a survey/mapping instrument, not a user sensor.** GoodMaps builds its indoor maps with "state-of-the-art LiDAR imaging and camera-based technology" ([GoodMaps](https://goodmaps.com/)); Waymap's team "walks your venue with LiDAR scanners, capturing a precise 3D point cloud" ([Waymap](https://www.waymapnav.com/our-tech)); Lazarillo lists "3D lidar scanning" as a mapping hardware option ([Lazarillo Business](https://lazarillo.app/business/)). The *user* then navigates with a phone camera (VPS) and inertial dead reckoning — no LiDAR on the user.
3. **Depth sensing that is not LiDAR.** The most credible new hardware uses stereo/IR depth or multi-camera rigs: Glide uses "dual infrared-enhanced stereo depth cameras" ([Glidance](https://www.glidance.io/product)); .lumen uses six cameras, two infrared laser projectors and three IMUs ([New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/wearables/dotlumen-ai-glasses-blind-independence/)); biped NOA uses three 170° cameras plus GPS ([biped FAQ](https://biped.ai/faq)).
4. **Ultrasonic remains the dominant *shipping* obstacle sensor.** WeWALK Smart Cane 2 uses "ultrasonic time-of-flight (ToF) sensors" plus a 6-axis IMU ([Electronics360, CES 2025](https://electronics360.globalspec.com/article/21809/wewalk-unveils-smarter-tdk-powered-cane-at-ces-2025)); SmartCane, BuzzClip, Miniguide and Ray are all ultrasonic.

**Only one commercially available product found that explicitly combines LiDAR + ultrasonic on the body:** Strap Tech's **Ara**, whose FAQ states it uses "an array of LiDar and ultrasonic sensors" ([Strap Tech FAQ](https://www.strap.tech/frequently-asked-questions)).

### Detail

**Apple Magnifier – Detection Mode.** Door Detection "helps users locate a door upon arriving at a new destination, understand how far they are from it, and describe door attributes"; it reads signs and symbols including room numbers and accessible-entrance symbols, and states whether a door opens "by pushing, turning a knob, or pulling a handle." Sensing is "LiDAR, camera, and on-device machine learning," and availability is restricted to "iPhone and iPad models with the LiDAR Scanner" — footnoted as iPhone 13 Pro/Pro Max, iPhone 12 Pro/Pro Max and iPad Pro ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2022/05/apple-previews-innovative-accessibility-features/)). The shipping user guide adds People Detection (speaks distance to a person), Furniture Detection (chairs/sofas, whether occupied, Announce Seat Occupancy), and multimodal feedback via "sounds, speech, and haptic feedback" that "becomes more frequent as you get closer"; it notes detection "is only available on supported iPhone models" ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/detect-doors-people-and-furniture-around-you-iph35c335575/ios)). In iOS 26 the same capability surfaces inside VoiceOver as **Live Recognition** — "scan your surroundings and get live descriptions of the scenes detected in the camera view," covering "scenes, people, doors, furniture, or text," plus Point & Speak ([Apple Support, iOS 26](https://support.apple.com/en-za/guide/iphone/iph37e6b3844/26/ios/26)). Apple also extended **Magnifier to Mac** (announced 13 May 2025), using Continuity Camera or attached USB cameras, noting Magnifier has existed on iPhone/iPad "since 2016" ([Apple Newsroom, May 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/05/apple-unveils-powerful-accessibility-features-coming-later-this-year/)).
*Limitations:* hardware-gated to Pro LiDAR models for door detection; Apple does not state indoor/outdoor suitability.

**Super Lidar – Lidar for Blind** (Mediate, "an MIT-spinoff AI start-up based in Boston"). Uses "LiDAR sensors" plus camera and AI to "parse the depth of the physical space," "scans and maps the 3D layout of your environment," determines distances, and gives audio feedback where "higher-pitch sound corresponds to longer distances" plus iPhone haptics whose "vibration strength increases with the size of the obstacle." Also detects people, their distance, and whether they wear masks — a COVID-era artifact. Free; 15.9 MB; version 1.2.1 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/super-lidar-lidar-for-blind/id1543706309)).
*Limitations:* residual social-distancing/mask framing suggests limited recent development; no indoor/outdoor guidance.

**Obstacle Detector – For Blind.** Measures distance "through the LiDAR scanner and TrueDepth camera equipped on the iPhone," with a stated maximum range of "up to 5 meters" and "centimeter-level error." Supported on "all Apple devices that support LiDAR scanners or feature Face ID." Sound/vibration proximity alerts, real-time distance speech, adjustable detection field of view and "creating a personalized 'blind cane'" are all **Premium-only** ([AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/apps/ios/navigation/obstacle-detector-blind)).

**AI Guide Dog.** "Uses the iPhone's LiDAR scanner or front camera for range measurement and azimuth detection"; adds a haptic/voice compass. Free, 843.8 KB, iOS 14+, and explicitly disclaims liability: "Please use the App in a safe environment. The developer does not bear any security responsibility" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-guide-dog/id6737235071)).

**EyeGuide Vision.** Markets itself as a "Free LiDAR & AR Navigation App": "Real-time LiDAR or AR based scanning warns of nearby objects," with turn-by-turn movement cues, scene description, smart reader (OCR), colour and currency detection, delivered via "clear voice prompts and gentle vibrations" ([eyeguidevision.org](https://eyeguidevision.org/)).

**Seeing AI / Be My Eyes / Envision App — no depth sensing.** These are the market-leading "AI eyes" apps but none document LiDAR or depth use. Seeing AI narrates text, documents with alignment cues, barcodes, accessible QR codes, currency, colour, ambient light, and photo exploration by touch ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.microsoft.seeingai&hl=en_US)). Be My AI "brings state-of-the-art visual descriptions" and is "all available for free through the Be My Eyes app," with human volunteers as fallback "for tougher requests" ([Be My Eyes](https://www.bemyeyes.com/bme-ai/)).
*Limitation for the LiDAR brief:* these tools answer "what is this?" but not "how far, and can I walk there?"

**Google Lookout.** Seven modes: Text, Explore (beta), Food labels, Documents, Currency, Images, Find. It "uses your device's camera and sensors to recognize objects and text," can give "direction and distance guidance to the object being looked for in most phones," and Explore mode "is still in beta and is less accurate than other modes" ([Google Support](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/9031274?hl=en)). At announcement Google recommended "wearing your Pixel device in a lanyard around your neck, or in your shirt pocket, with the camera pointing away from your body," and noted "the core experience is processed on the device" so it works offline ([Google Blog, 8 May 2018](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/accessibility/lookout-app-help-blind-and-visually-impaired-people-learn-about-their-surroundings/)).

### Detail

**WeWALK Smart Cane 2.** The CES 2025 launch article documents the sensor stack precisely: "The cane uses MEMS microphones, motion sensors and ultrasonic time-of-flight (ToF) sensors," with TDK's "InvenSense six-axis inertial measurement unit" and "InvenSense ultrasonic ToF sensor," plus an AI-powered voice assistant ([Electronics360](https://electronics360.globalspec.com/article/21809/wewalk-unveils-smarter-tdk-powered-cane-at-ces-2025)). TDK's own write-up confirms the "SmartMotion 6-axis IMU sensor," calls WeWALK "a UK-based startup," and lists Time Best Inventions 2023, the King's Awards for Enterprise Innovation 2024 and Edison Awards 2024; it notes "its modular design allows future hardware and software upgrades" ([TDK](https://www.tdk.com/en/featured_stories/entry_084-WeWALK-Smart-Cane-2.html)). RNIB's listing names the components — "built-in ultrasonic sensor technology," gyroscope, magnetometer, compass, pressure sensor, MEMS microphone, vibration motor — and says the cane "connects seamlessly to your smartphone via Bluetooth to provide turn-by-turn GPS navigation instructions" ([RNIB](https://shop.rnib.org.uk/products/wewalk-v2-smart-cane-grey)). WeWALK's own product page lists "Detect obstacle above chest level," clock-direction and turn-by-turn navigation, multimodal transit navigation, POI exploration, transit stop tracking with arrival notifications, and voice commands, connecting via "Bluetooth 4.2" ([WeWALK](https://wewalk.io/en/product-details/)). A US reseller spells out the physical spec: obstacle detection "above chest height, including lampposts, tree branches, truck mirrors, and street signs," "detection range can be set between 30 and 70 inches," "20 hour average battery life," "USB chargeable; 2 hour charging time," pairing with "an Ambutech graphite white cane," plus "AI integration with 4 years of built in Chat GPT" ([Independent Living Aids](https://independentliving.com/wewalk-ultrasonic-smartcane-v2-with-ai/); [LS&S](https://lssproducts.com/wewalk-smart-cane-v2-plus-with-ai/)). WeWALK claims it is "the only smart cane endorsed by CNIB, RNIB, and Vision Australia" ([WeWALK](https://wewalk.io/en/)).
*Limitations:* ultrasonic detection is confined to chest-height-and-above obstacles within ~0.75–1.8 m; AI features require phone connection ("cane must be connected to the WeWalk app to access AI"); the voice assistant is a paid subscription; no LiDAR and no true ground/drop-off sensing.

**SmartCane (India).** Clip-on unit that "can be mounted onto a traditional white cane," detects obstacles above knee height "such as a tree branch," and conveys range through "vibrations which will differ according to the distance of the object at hand." Detection: "up to 3 metres away outdoors and 1.8 metres indoors." Rechargeable lithium-ion, manuals "in various languages including Braille," and a training programme; Phoenix Medical manufactures, Saksham Trust distributes with welfare organisations ([IIT Delhi press release, 13 Feb 2014](https://assistech.iitd.ac.in/doc/Smartcane_Press_release.pdf)). E4C records CE marking, ISO-certified processes, "$90.00" MSRP and "over 20,000 SmartCanes … distributed as of 2019" ([E4C](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/solutions/product/smartcane/)).
*Limitations:* ultrasonic only; requires a host cane; distribution largely tied to Indian subsidy channels.

**Ray (Caretec).** "Ultrasonic technology" for obstacles beyond cane reach plus a "light sensor for environmental assessment, including to find an open door." Normal mode detects to 2.85 m, Escape mode to 1.7 m; Teacher/Student modes work over a cable link for O&M instruction; auto-off after 60 s; 60 g; 2×AAA ([Caretec](https://www.caretec.at/product/ray-powered-mobility-guide/)).

**Miniguide.** Explicitly positioned as a *secondary* aid: "designed to provide information to a blind traveller which will supplement that provided by other aids, such as the long cane or guide dog," giving "extra non critical information," with gap-finding ranges to distinguish "door openings." Warnings: "Only use the Miniguide in a safe and familiar environment" and "the Miniguide is not water proof" ([GDP Research](https://www.gdp-research.com.au/minig_4.htm)).

### Detail

**Ara (Strap Tech)** is the clearest commercial LiDAR wearable found. The FAQ states Ara is "a wearable smart mobility aid for the blind and visually impaired" that uses "an array of LiDar and ultrasonic sensors" and identifies obstacles at three heights — "low (feet to waist)," "mid (waist to shoulders)," "top (shoulders to head)" — and differentiates "stairs going up," "stairs going down," and "recessed obstacles, like gaps and holes." Feedback is a "simple and intuitive haptic language" where "each type of obstacle has a distinct and unique vibration pattern," with a White Cane mode for use alongside other aids ([Strap Tech FAQ](https://www.strap.tech/frequently-asked-questions)). The product page confirms sensors "continuously scan your surroundings" and provide "a comprehensive view of the world around you," worn on an "adjustable and stretchable chest harness" in Small and Large sizes ([Strap Tech](https://www.strap.tech/product/ara-device)).
*Limitations:* "Ara's array of sensors needs to be uncovered at all times to work properly"; "at this time, ARA does not work with 3rd party GPS apps"; supply is capped at 250 units; the pricing page shows only financing instalments, not a headline cash price; the Ara device page does not name the company or state availability.

**.lumen glasses.** Positioned as a guide-dog substitute: "the world's first technology that replicates the functionality of a guide dog… If a guide dog pulls your hand to show you where to go, the .lumen glasses do the same through gentle vibrations on your forehead." They "compute 100 times a second where you can safely walk," detect obstacles "above or below the ground," and help avoid "roads, puddles, mud"; destination navigation is listed as coming soon ([.lumen](https://www.dotlumen.com/glasses)). Independent reporting details the hardware — "six cameras," "two infrared laser projectors for operating after dark," "three inertial measurement units," and "GPS systems" — plus €9,999 reservations, CE certification for European marketing, "more than 400 blind users across 40+ countries" testing, and the caveat that "it might be a while before it's okayed for sale stateside" ([New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/wearables/dotlumen-ai-glasses-blind-independence/)). In Romania, over 1,500 people preordered "free of charge" through "a national government program supporting people with disabilities," making Romania "the first country in the world where glasses that guide the blind are made accessible on a large scale" ([.lumen](https://www.dotlumen.com/post/over-1-500-preorders-for-lumen-glasses-in-just-10-days)).
*Limitations:* price is the highest in the category; no US regulatory clearance; head-mounted haptics require training; .lumen's own homepage discloses no sensor specifics ([.lumen](https://www.dotlumen.com/)).

**biped NOA.** "The world's first AI-powered mobility vest designed specifically for people who are blind or have low vision," worn "like a backpack." Three ultra-wide cameras each with a 170° field of view give "360-degree obstacle detection"; feedback is "3D spatial beeps that intensify as you approach obstacles, with left/right and top/bottom positioning" via "advanced bone conduction headphones." GPS is "enhanced with cameras," powered by "Google Gemini & Google Maps," with live AI scene monitoring, object finding, text/sign reading and bus-stop detection ([biped](https://biped.ai/home); [biped FAQ](https://biped.ai/faq)). Explicitly complementary: "we designed NOA as a complement, that does not interfere with the white cane technique" ([biped](https://www.biped.ai/get-noa/)). Package includes two batteries for 6 hours, charger, travel bag, headphones, updates, support and app access.
*Limitations:* no LiDAR or depth camera documented — distance estimation is vision-derived; cost is high; requires connectivity for "real-time AI processing."

**Envision Glasses and the Google Glass problem.** Envision Glasses are "built on Google Glass Enterprise Edition 2" with "an 8 MP camera with a wide field of view," delivering Instant Text, Scan Text, Batch Scan, Layout Detection, Ask Envision (GPT-5), Describe Scene, Explore, light detection, banknote and colour recognition, Call a Companion and Aira calling on Home/Professional editions ([Envision](https://www.letsenvision.com/glasses/home)). The "Call an Ally" hands-free video-calling feature is Envision's differentiator ([Envision Ally](https://www.letsenvision.com/ally)). Store prices remain live at $1,899/$2,499/$3,499 ([Envision Store](https://shop.letsenvision.com/collections/envision-glasses)). But Google confirmed it was no longer selling Glass Enterprise Edition 2 and ended support, software updates and repair service on 15 September 2023, noting "the devices should still work after that date" but without updates ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2023/3/15/23641872/google-glass-enterprise-edition-discontinued-support)). Envision's strategic response is **Ally Solos** — "the first truly wearable AI glasses built for accessibility," <50 g, splash-resistant, 15-hour battery with quick-swap stems, IP67, at $399 launch pricing including a year of Ally Pro ([Envision](https://www.letsenvision.com/blog/ally-solos-glasses); [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/759160/ally-solos-smart-glasses-ai-envision-low-vision)).
*Limitation:* neither Envision product has any depth sensing; both are reading/description devices, not mobility devices.

**OrCam MyEye 3 Pro.** Camera-and-AI reading/recognition device controlled by "voice commands" and "simple hand gestures," covering Smart Reading, magnification, translation, "Just Ask" AI Q&A, face recognition, barcode product ID, colour, banknotes, and — relevant here — "identifying nearby objects and their relative locations with the **beta** Orientation feature" ([AdaptiVision](https://lowvisionsource.com/product/orcam-myeye-3-pro/)). OrCam's own shop lists MyEye 3 Pro at $4,490 regular / $4,250 sale, MyEye Smart $3,500, OrCam Read 3 $2,790 ([Get OrCam](https://www.getorcam.com/buy-now)).
*Limitations:* no depth sensing; spatial orientation is beta-quality; price point among the highest for a non-mobility device.

**eSight Go.** A visual-enhancement (not description) device for "central vision loss": "an advanced high-speed, high-definition camera to capture continuous video footage," processed by "clinically-validated smart algorithms" and shown on "two HD OLED screens," with a trackpad for "zoom, contrast and focus controls" ([eSight FAQ](https://www.esighteyewear.com/faq/)). $4,950, "available now," with lifetime software upgrades and five coaching sessions ([AdaptiVision](https://lowvisionsource.com/product/esight-go/)).
*Limitations:* aimed at residual-vision users, not the totally blind; camera FOV (68.8° H) exceeds display FOV (38.8° H); no LiDAR or obstacle logic.

**Sunu Band — a cautionary case.** Technically it was an ultrasonic/sonar wristband detecting objects "up to 5.5m or 16ft away," with vibration pulses that "become more frequent as an object is close," a haptic compass, place finder and GPS navigation, and Premium sonar modes for "Hardness" and "Shape" ([VisionAid](https://www.visionaid.co.uk/sunu-band-premium)); APH describes ultrasonic detection "up to 14 ft. away" with gesture-recognition control ([APH](https://www.aph.org/product/sunu-band/)). In June 2023, founder/CEO Marco Trujillo wrote that Sunu had "no alternative but to cease operations due to financial insolvency," could not "provide refunds or product replacements," would maintain the app only through the rest of 2023 before transferring it to a partner with "no further development," and unlocked "all premium features … for all users" ([Sunu CEO letter](https://milled.com/sunu-band/an-important-update-from-sunus-ceo-n7V4WEajj9Q9VYbH)). Both APH and VisionAid now list the product as discontinued.

**Be My Eyes on Meta glasses.** Volunteers "'see-through' the camera of your smart glasses and provide audible feedback directly through the open-ear speakers," with seamless switching to the phone camera "to read some fine print or for a high resolution wide-angle view" ([Be My Eyes](https://www.bemyeyes.com/be-my-eyes-smartglasses/)). The March 2026 expansion adds hands-free connection to trusted groups and to brand service teams via "Hey Meta, Be My Eyes with [name of group or company]," for tasks like "configuring broadband equipment," "navigating a hotel room," and "locating products in-store," at no cost to the glasses user ([Be My Eyes](https://www.bemyeyes.com/news/be-my-eyes-and-meta-launch-new-accessibility-functions/)). Meta's accessibility page confirms the camera + "multi-microphone array" basis and notes prices "range depending on the style" ([Meta](https://www.meta.com/ai-glasses/blind-visually-impaired/)).
*Limitation:* purely camera/AI/human — no depth or ranging; requires connectivity.

### Detail

**GoodMaps** is the strongest LiDAR-in-the-stack indoor platform: "Using state-of-the-art LiDAR imaging and camera-based technology, GoodMaps provides high accuracy down to the foot," with "No Hardware, Zero Installations" and "infrastructure-free indoor navigation" across transportation, retail, corporate, recreation, healthcare, education, community and government venues ([GoodMaps](https://goodmaps.com/)). The consumer app is "available on iOS and Android and free to all users," offers AR view and accuracy "down to the meter," and requires ARKit/ARCore functionality ([GoodMaps app](https://goodmaps.com/the-app/)). The Explore app on Google Play adds location-aware callouts (direction, nearest room, street, POIs) at configurable 10/30/60/120-second intervals, though that listing shows a last update of 5 Jan 2023 and only 1K+ downloads ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.goodmaps.explore)). Deployment evidence: a Walmart Mexico pilot at the Torres Lindavista store, with the relationship beginning "in late 2021" via three-phase testing at the Bentonville Associate Store ([GoodMaps](https://goodmaps.com/newsroom/walmart-mexico-partners-with-goodmaps-to-offer-a-digital-navigation-experience/)).
*Limitations:* venues must be LiDAR-surveyed first; app requires an AR-capable phone; the older Explore listing suggests fragmentation between app generations.

**Waymap** inverts the sensing model: LiDAR is used only to build the map, while the user's phone needs no signal at all. "Waymap's SmartStep is a proprietary algorithm that uses only the motion sensors already inside a standard smartphone… no GPS, no WiFi, no Bluetooth, no mobile signal required," fusing accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer and barometer "using particle filtration, delivering 100x/second." This is described as "AI-powered dead reckoning," and "SmartStep learns each individual user's gait, so accuracy improves over time." The initial fix comes from a camera-based VPS accurate to "centimeter-level," built from an "AI Visual Fingerprint" — and "no images are stored" ([Waymap](https://www.waymapnav.com/our-tech)). Washington DC is "the first entire city and transport network to become available on Waymap's platform" ([Waymap](https://www.waymapnav.com/newsroom/waymap-goes-live-in-washington-dc)).
*Limitations:* requires professional LiDAR survey per venue; needs a starting-position fix; coverage is still city/venue-by-venue.

**NaviLens** is marker-based, and its performance envelope is what makes it work for blind users: "instant reading up to 12 times farther than a QR," "no need to focus, in any lighting condition, at angles up to 160°," reading distance "+30 m," detection time "1/30s," 42 languages, with augmented-reality arrows, audio cues and haptic vibration ([NaviLens](https://www.navilens.com/en)). Google Play describes it as "a high-density artificial markers system for long distance reading" whose tags are readable "without the need of focussing and even in motion," developed over five years by NAVILENS TECH S.L. in Murcia ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.neosistec.NaviLens&hl=en_US)).
*Limitation:* requires physical code installation — zero function in unmarked space.

**RightHear** is the beacon-infrastructure model: "a mobile app, beacons to be installed in the venue, and an online admin portal (Dashboard) for managing the audio description and fleet." At each Accessible Spot "a Bluetooth sensor beacon" transmits information to the phone; Outdoor Mode "extracts the information from an open source map (GPS)" ([RightHear FAQ](https://www.right-hear.com/faq-english/)). Its own site calls the product "talking signage" / an "Audio Format Signage Orientation System," with deployments including a safari park, a university campus (University of Alaska Anchorage, "official launch planned for the start of UAA's 2024-25 academic year"), hotels and restaurants ([RightHear](https://www.right-hear.com/en/)).
*Limitations:* beacon batteries need replacing after four years; DIY installs capped at three beacons; recurring multi-year subscription per venue.

**BlindSquare** remains the most widely deployed paid app. It "uses GPS and the compass to locate you," pulls venue data "in real time from Foursquare and OpenStreetMap," reports "interesting places in front of the user," next transit stops and street crossings as you pass them ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/blindsquare/id500557255)). Indoors it uses "Bluetooth beacons … to silently identify key locations such as doorways, stairs, and hallway junctures" plus "BlindSquare QR Blazes" for fixed-location audio info such as "elevator call buttons, meeting rooms or class rooms, restrooms, building directories," with "access to over 125M POI's globally" outdoors ([BlindSquare](https://www.blindsquare.com/indoor/)).

**Aira** is the human-in-the-loop layer rather than a sensing product: users "ask questions, get clarification, and even verify the AI responses with a human visual interpreter for added confidence," for tasks from "reading mail" to "navigating new environments," with free use at participating retail locations ([Aira](https://aira.io/subscriptions/)). Retail pricing is deliberately opaque: "the price list shows the full retail price however, this is not what an individual will pay," because Aira applies "Aira Offsets" set per plan type ([Aira](https://aira.io/new-plans/)). Aira also integrates as a callable service inside Envision Glasses Home and Professional editions ([Envision](https://www.letsenvision.com/glasses/home)).

**Microsoft Soundscape (discontinued)** is the most important design precedent in this category. It "used 3D audio cues to enrich ambient awareness and provided a new way to relate to the environment," "unlike step-by-step navigation apps," running "in the background in conjunction with navigation or other applications," letting users "move toward a self-set audio beacon," and "supporting individuals in making mental maps and personal route choices" ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/product/soundscape/)). It was discontinued as of 3 Jan 2023 with existing installs usable until 30 Aug 2023; features included Street Preview, Markers, Annotations and Siri Shortcuts, and it was used for "remote instruction by Orientation & Mobility specialists" ([Microsoft support](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/product/soundscape/support/)). Notably, Glidance founder **Amos Miller** is described as "a blind technologist who pioneered spatial augmented reality at Microsoft Research and led Microsoft Soundscape" ([Glidance FAQ](https://glidance.io/frequently-asked-questions/)) — direct lineage from the discontinued app to the leading new robotic aid.

### Detail

**Glide (Glidance)** is the most commercially advanced robotic aid. It is "the world's first intelligent guide for people who are blind or have low vision," "a two-wheeled, AI-powered primary mobility aid" positioned as "a third primary mobility option alongside the white cane and guide dog," which "actively steers and guides your path, handling obstacles and navigation autonomously rather than requiring constant manual interpretation" ([Glidance FAQ](https://glidance.io/frequently-asked-questions/)). Sensing is explicitly stereo depth, not LiDAR: "dual infrared-enhanced stereo depth cameras" plus near-range ground and overhead obstacle detection, cliff detection, and "broader vision up to 50 feet ahead"; "an array of advanced cameras and sensors work together to physically guide you around obstacles, locate targets like doors, stairs, and elevators, and get you to your destination safely." Navigation is branded "Agentic Wayfinding™," powered by "a purpose-built navigation model for complex, unstructured pedestrian environments" ([Glidance](https://www.glidance.io/product)). Prototype lineage is public: "Hugo, Ada, Marie, DeLorien, Galileo, and Rover (the Alpha 2.0, named by the community)" ([Glidance FAQ](https://glidance.io/frequently-asked-questions/)). As of 16 July 2026, "our Pilot Rollout has begun," with Founding Gliders walking solo on "their own routes," pre-production units carrying "a more powerful chip" and "a new electronics platform," and one-on-one in-person onboarding ([Glidance news](https://www.glidance.io/news/glide-july-2026-updates)).
*Limitations and schedule risk:* originally announced for a "2025" launch when preorders opened in July 2024 ([Glidance, July 2024](https://www.glidance.io/news/glidance-launches-pre-orders-for-glide-its-long-awaited-self-guided-mobility-aid)), now on an "updated Spring 2026 production timeline" with the roadmap page itself under revision; "at launch, Glide will ship with Freestyle Mode" while **Directed Navigation is "coming post-launch via an over-the-air (OTA) software update"** and user-trained object recognition "will come in future updates"; orders are closed with a waitlist; the $30/month connectivity subscription is mandatory for LTE/updates.

**Milo** is the cost-disruption datapoint. Described as "the first fully autonomous robot guide dog," it is "fully autonomous," "requiring no a priori knowledge of the environment," and "completely self-contained with all computation performed onboard," built on a modified Unitree Go2 for "approximately $2k USD" — against guide dogs at "approximately $50k USD plus ongoing costs" ([arXiv:2607.19530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19530)). Mila adds that its "locally-run AI model also allows it to operate completely on-device without relying on an internet connection or external servers," using "an abstract bird's-eye view (BEV) representation," "powerful segmentation models such as SAM," and reinforcement learning in "a custom, GPU-accelerated simulator" that "randomizes paths, walls, obstacles, and pedestrians"; Milo "keeps a memory of what the user has encountered before while maintaining a consistent map of its surroundings" ([Mila](https://mila.quebec/en/article/milo-the-first-fully-autonomous-robot-guide-dog)). Coverage frames it as "a lower-cost alternative while people wait for a trained guide dog," able to "help users travel through unfamiliar locations without a prior 3D scan," and released as "an open-source platform" with public hardware designs, simulation software and pretrained models ([Robotics & Automation News](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/08/04/researchers-unveil-open-source-robotic-guide-dog-designed-to-assist-blind-and-visually-impaired-people/103878/)).
*Limitations:* research prototype; "could be manufactured" for <$2,000 but is not in production; quadruped form factor implies noise, battery and social-acceptance issues not addressed in the sources.

**RoboGuide** targets the conversational/indoor-docent use case: sensors "accurately map and assess its surroundings," software helps the system "learn the optimal routes between locations" and "interpret the sensor data in real-time" to avoid moving obstacles, and it "incorporates large language model technology" to answer users' "questions and comments" with "verbal responses in return" — trialled for museums, shopping centres and hospitals with FVSC and RNIB Scotland ([University of Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/archiveofnews/2024/february/headline_1043333_en.html)). Binghamton's parallel work uses LLMs to give "plan verbalization" before departure and "scene verbalization" during travel — e.g. announcing "this is a long corridor" ([Binghamton](https://www.binghamton.edu/news/story/6168/these-ai-powered-guide-dogs-dont-just-lead-they-talk)).

## Cross-cutting analysis and implications

**1. LiDAR's real commercial role is mapping, not mobility.** Three of the four leading indoor platforms (GoodMaps, Waymap, Lazarillo) use LiDAR to survey venues and then localise users with camera VPS, markers, beacons or inertial dead reckoning. For a project on "mapped and unmapped LiDAR environments," this is the decisive market split: **mapped** = GoodMaps/Waymap-style point-cloud survey + phone localisation; **unmapped** = Apple LiDAR Detection Mode, Ara, Glide, Milo — all doing live geometry without a prior map. Milo's explicit claim of navigating "without a prior 3D scan" ([Robotics & Automation News](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/08/04/researchers-unveil-open-source-robotic-guide-dog-designed-to-assist-blind-and-visually-impaired-people/103878/)) is the clearest statement of the unmapped frontier.

**2. Phone LiDAR is an underexploited platform.** The four third-party iOS LiDAR apps found are all free, tiny (843 KB–16 MB), single-purpose proximity buzzers with limited documentation, no indoor/outdoor guidance and, in one case, an explicit liability disclaimer. Apple's own implementation is materially better (semantic door attributes, sign reading, haptic gradient) but hardware-gated to Pro models. Range is the ceiling: 5 m stated for Obstacle Detector ([AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/apps/ios/navigation/obstacle-detector-blind)) versus 15 m for Glide's stereo vision.

**3. Feedback grammar is where products differentiate, not sensors.** Distinct approaches: pitch-mapped audio (Super Lidar), increasing-frequency haptics (Apple, BuzzClip, Sunu), 3D spatial beeps with left/right and up/down positioning (biped NOA), obstacle-type-specific vibration patterns at three body heights (Ara), forehead-pull haptics mimicking a guide dog harness (.lumen), and physical steering of the user's hand (Glide). Ara's "haptic language" and .lumen's guide-dog metaphor are the two most transferable interaction models.

**4. Price is the binding constraint.** Effective mobility-grade hardware sits at $1,499–€9,999. Only two mechanisms have broken that: state procurement (.lumen free to Romanian users via a national disability programme) and India's ADIP subsidy channel for SmartCane (20,000+ units). Milo's ~$2,000 open-source bill of materials is the third potential path.

**5. Business-model fragility is a real risk factor.** Sunu (insolvency), BAWA (component EOL), Soundscape (project conclusion), Google Glass EE2 (platform withdrawal, stranding Envision Glasses buyers who paid up to $3,499) and Glide's slip from a 2025 to a Spring 2026 production timeline all argue for designing prototypes on durable platforms — mainstream phones, open standards (Wayfindr), or open-source stacks (Soundscape code, Milo) rather than bespoke hardware.

**6. Gaps no commercial product credibly fills.** (a) Drop-offs, kerbs and descending stairs — only Ara ("stairs going down," "recessed obstacles, like gaps and holes") and Glide ("cliff detection") claim these. (b) Glass and mirrors — no commercial product addresses this; it appears only as MIT Media Lab research on "detecting and mapping transparent or mirror-like surfaces with LiDAR." (c) Unmapped indoor wayfinding — every indoor platform requires prior survey, markers or beacons; Clew's self-recorded routes are the only user-generated alternative found, and it "seems that you can only record one path at a time" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clew/id1268077870)).
